How to actually choose

  • Just keeping your phone alive? 10,000mAh is genuinely enough — don't overspend on capacity you won't use.
  • Frequent long outages? Go 20,000mAh, and prioritize one with fast charging so it doesn't take all day to refill between cuts.
  • Charging a laptop or multiple devices? Only the 20,000-30,000mAh range with the right output will actually manage it — check the wattage, not just the mAh number.
  • Flying anywhere with it? Ghanaian aviation authorities follow IATA rules — power banks over 27,000mAh (100Wh) are banned from carry-on entirely, and 20,000-27,000mAh units need airline approval. Anything under 20,000mAh travels freely in carry-on, but never in checked baggage.

Watch out for counterfeits

Ghana's Energy Commission and the National Communications Authority don't certify or test power banks sold locally, which means counterfeit units can and do bypass import checks — especially common in markets like Circle or Kantamanto. Buying from a verified retailer with buyer protection (like Jumia) rather than an unverified street vendor is the single best way to avoid a bank that swells, overheats, or just quietly stops holding a charge after a month.

Frequently asked questions

What mAh power bank do I actually need?

For getting a phone through a normal dumsor outage, 10,000mAh (roughly 2 full charges) is enough for most people. If you're also charging a laptop, running a router, or dealing with longer outages, step up to 20,000mAh or more.

Is fast charging worth paying extra for?

If your phone supports it, yes — it can mean the difference between a 3-hour and a 1-hour full charge. Check your phone's supported wattage first, since a fast-charging power bank won't help if your phone caps out at a lower speed anyway.

How do I know if a power bank is fake?

Fakes often list a much higher mAh than the battery inside can actually deliver, feel unusually light for their stated capacity, or heat up fast while charging.
