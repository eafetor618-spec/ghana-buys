Best Data Bundles in Ghana

Last verified Aug 2026 · 6 min read

There are really only two networks worth comparing now, not three — AT (formerly AirtelTigo) is being folded into Telecel. Here's what that actually means for your data, plus how to stop losing money to auto-renewal.

What changed: The government-driven merger of AT Ghana (AirtelTigo) into Telecel was announced in September 2025, with AT subscribers already moved onto Telecel's network on a roaming basis shortly after. If you're still on an AT SIM, your service should continue working through the transition — but for a new line today, MTN or Telecel are the only two real choices.

MTN

Best for coverage

Ghana's largest network by a wide margin, and it shows most outside the big cities — if you travel between regions or live somewhere Telecel's signal gets patchy, MTN is the safer default. Bundles tend to cost a little more per gigabyte than Telecel's equivalent tiers, but you're paying for reach: the widest 4G footprint in the country, with 5G rolling out in more neighborhoods.

Pros
  • Widest 4G/5G coverage nationwide
  • Most reliable in rural areas
  • Largest range of bundle sizes
Cons
  • Usually pricier per GB than Telecel
  • Popular bundles can slow down at peak hours in dense areas

Telecel

Best value for money

Usually the cheaper option per gigabyte, especially on weekly and monthly bundles, and now absorbing AT's former customers and spectrum — coverage should keep improving as the merger completes, particularly in areas where AT already had a presence. Worth a look even if you've never used Telecel before.

Pros
  • Generally best price per GB
  • Coverage expanding post-merger
  • Strong presence in Kumasi
Cons
  • Signal can still lag MTN in remote areas
  • Merger integration is ongoing — expect some rough edges

AT (formerly AirtelTigo)

Being absorbed into Telecel

Existing AT SIMs continue to work, roaming onto Telecel's network, but AT is not really a standalone option anymore. There's no good reason to buy a new AT SIM today — if you want what AT offered, you're better off going straight to Telecel.

Pros
  • Existing customers stay connected during the transition
Cons
  • Not available as a fresh choice going forward
  • Long-term plan is full integration into Telecel

How to actually choose

  • Live or travel outside Accra/Kumasi often? MTN's coverage advantage is worth the extra cost per GB.
  • Mostly stay in one city and want to save money? Compare Telecel's current bundle prices against MTN's — Telecel is usually cheaper for the same data.
  • Need a home internet connection, not just phone data? Both MTN and Telecel offer fixed 4G/fiber home broadband in Accra, Kumasi and a growing list of other cities — availability and pricing vary a lot by neighborhood, so check coverage at your actual address before committing to either.
  • Still on an AT SIM? No need to rush and switch — but if you're buying a new line specifically, go with Telecel directly rather than AT.

Watch out for auto-renewal

Daily and weekly bundles on all networks typically auto-renew by default — if you don't use up the bundle and forget to switch this off, the network quietly deducts the same amount again from your balance at the next cycle. This is one of the most common ways people in Ghana lose money on data without realizing it. Check your network's app or dial your standard balance/bundle menu to see your active bundles and turn off auto-renewal on anything you didn't mean to keep repeating.

Frequently asked questions

Should I switch from AT to Telecel now?

If you're a current AT customer, there's no urgent need — your line keeps working through the transition. If you're buying a brand new SIM, go with Telecel directly instead of AT, since that's where things are heading anyway.

Why does my data bundle keep charging me automatically?

Auto-renewal is on by default for most daily and weekly bundles across all three networks. Check your active bundles in your network's app or USSD menu and switch off auto-renewal for any bundle you don't want repeating.

Is MTN worth the extra cost over Telecel?

If you mostly stay within a city with good Telecel coverage, probably not — you're better off saving the money. If you travel between regions or live somewhere with patchier signal, MTN's wider coverage tends to be worth paying more for.
