Best Online Stores to Shop From in Ghana

Last verified Aug 2026 · 6 min read

Online shopping in Ghana isn't one-size-fits-all — the right store depends on what you're buying and how much you trust the seller. Here's how the eight main options actually compare.

Jumia

#1 overall

Ghana's largest online retailer, and the safest default for electronics and phones specifically. Cash on delivery, buyer protection, and frequent flash sales (their Anniversary Sale and Black Friday events regularly bring the deepest discounts of the year) make it the store with the least risk if something goes wrong.

Pros
  • Cash on delivery
  • Buyer protection on disputes
  • Huge selection
  • Frequent flash sales
Cons
  • Delivery slower on rural addresses
  • Third-party sellers vary in quality
Shop Jumia →

Tonaton

Best for local, used items

A classifieds-style marketplace rather than a retailer — good for buying used items, negotiating directly with a seller, or finding something local without shipping. No buyer protection though, so you're relying entirely on your own judgment when meeting a seller.

Pros
  • Great for used/local goods
  • Direct price negotiation
  • No shipping wait
Cons
  • No buyer protection
  • Scam risk with unknown sellers

Melcom Online

Best for household + appliances

Melcom's physical store reputation carries over online — a reliable option for home appliances, furniture, and household goods where you want an established Ghanaian brand behind the purchase rather than a marketplace of third-party sellers.

Pros
  • Trusted established brand
  • Good for appliances/home goods
  • Physical stores as backup
Cons
  • Smaller electronics selection than Jumia

Telefonika

Best for phone specialists

A more specialized option focused specifically on phones and accessories. Worth checking alongside Jumia when phone-shopping specifically — sometimes better on specific models Jumia doesn't stock, worth a price comparison before you commit.

Pros
  • Phone-focused selection
  • Sometimes better model availability
Cons
  • Smaller than Jumia overall
  • Less buyer protection infrastructure

Jiji

Best for verified used goods

Similar to Tonaton in concept — new and used items, direct seller negotiation — but with a verification system that helps flag more trustworthy sellers. Popular for phones, laptops, and fashion specifically, and generally seen as a safer classifieds option than Tonaton for exactly that reason.

Pros
  • Seller verification system
  • Good for used phones/laptops
  • Direct negotiation
Cons
  • Still buyer-beware overall
  • Meet in safe public locations only

Compu Ghana

Best for genuine electronics + warranty

A dedicated electronics retailer specializing in phones, laptops, tablets and accessories, with genuine products and manufacturer warranties. They also have physical stores for pickup, which matters if you want to see or test a device before committing.

Pros
  • Genuine products, real warranties
  • Physical pickup option
  • Specialist electronics focus
Cons
  • Narrower catalogue than Jumia

Palace

Best for hire-purchase on big appliances

A long-established Ghanaian retailer for home improvement and electronics, with an online store extending their physical showrooms. Their hire-purchase/financing options are the standout — useful if you're buying something big like a fridge or TV and don't want to pay the full price upfront.

Pros
  • Hire-purchase financing available
  • Established, trusted brand
  • Good for big-ticket appliances
Cons
  • Less useful for small everyday purchases

Shopbeta

Best for daily deal hunting

A general merchandise marketplace that aggregates products from multiple suppliers, similar in spirit to Jumia but smaller. Their "Deal of the Day" section is worth checking specifically — regularly features discounts on electronics and fashion that can beat Jumia's regular pricing.

Pros
  • Frequent daily deals
  • Broad catalogue for its size
Cons
  • Smaller than Jumia, less buyer protection infrastructure

Bottom line

For anything electronics or phones, start with Jumia — the buyer protection alone is worth it. For used items or local deals, Tonaton is unmatched, just be careful who you're dealing with. For household goods and appliances, Melcom's brand trust is hard to beat.

Frequently asked questions

Is Jumia actually legit in Ghana?

Yes — it's the largest and most established online retailer in the country, with real buyer protection, returns, and (in most areas) pay-on-delivery. That's exactly why it's our top overall pick.

Can I pay cash on delivery on these sites?

Jumia offers pay-on-delivery in most major areas. Smaller stores vary — some support it, others require mobile money or card payment upfront, so check at checkout before assuming.

What if my order never arrives or shows up damaged?

With Jumia, contact their support through the app or "Chat With Us" — they have a formal returns/refund process. With smaller or classifieds-style stores like Tonaton, there's far less formal protection, so only pay in person after inspecting the item where possible.
